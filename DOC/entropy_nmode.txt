  RRHO ENTROPY CALCULATIONS 

See https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jcim.8b00805 
    https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.1195
and references cited therein.

The translational– rotational–vibrational entropy terms
for each minimzed structure are computed with the well-known
statistical-thermodynamic formulas as implemented in NMODE.
After separating the translational motion of the center
of mass and the internal motions, the statistical
formulas are derived by introducing the rigid
rotor-harmonic oscillator (RRHO) approximation,
which splits the internal motions into rotational
and vibrational contributions.
These entropy contributions depend on
fundamental constants and on molecular parameters
(total mass , moments of inertia and harmonic vibrational
frequencies.  In addition, the translational term depends on 
the standard state which is specified by the volume V occupied
by the N molecules. The sum of these entropic
contributions gives an estimate of the third-law
entropy,

     S_RRHO = S_trans + S_rot + S_vib

which can be denoted as the RRHO entropy owing to the essential 
role played by the RRHO approximation in its derivation.

The RRHO entropies of solute molecules in aqueous solution are calculated
with the MM potential used during the MD simulations. A set of 500-1000 
equally-spaced MD snapshots is normally considered for the calculations . 
The structures must be subject to geometry optimization setting a 
tight tolerance in the energy gradient, but avoiding that the minima 
deviate significantly from the sampled structures in solution. 
To this end, the solvated MD snapshots were truncated by keeping 
the coordinates of the solute atoms and those of a buffer layer of 
waters with a ∼8 Å thickness around the solute atoms. 
The sander program is used to relax the internal geometry of the solute, 
until the RMSD of the elements in the gradient vector is less than 
1E-6  kcal/( mol Å ). Subsequently, the calculation of the
Hessian matrix of second derivatives and its diagonalization to
obtain the normal modes are  restricted to the active region
comprising the solute atoms using a locally modified version of
the NMODE program. A similar protocol has been shown to 
reduce the statistical uncertainty of the entropy terms 
and give improved MM/PBSA scorings.


The final RRHO entropy corresponds to the simple average 
of S_RRHO over the set of structures with a statistical uncertainty 
given by the standard error of the mean.  Interestingly,
Reasonably converged values of S_RRHO can be calculated considering only 
around 100 snapshots.
